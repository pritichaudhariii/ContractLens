# Proxy Statement DEF 14A 2026 Annual Meeting (Northwind Analytics)

UNITED STATES SECURITIES AND EXCHANGE COMMISSION. SCHEDULE 14A. Proxy Statement Pursuant to Section 14(a) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934. Filed by the Registrant. Definitive Proxy Statement. Northwind Analytics, Inc. This document is an excerpt of the definitive proxy statement for the 2026 annual meeting of stockholders.

## Notice of 2026 Annual Meeting of Stockholders

The 2026 annual meeting of stockholders of Northwind Analytics, Inc. will be held on Thursday, May 14, 2026 at 10:00 a.m. Central Time. The meeting will be held in a virtual-only format via live audio webcast at www.virtualshareholdermeeting.example/NWND2026; there will be no physical location. Stockholders of record at the close of business on March 20, 2026 (the "Record Date") are entitled to notice of and to vote at the meeting. As of the Record Date there were 73,842,115 shares of common stock outstanding, each entitled to one vote. The holders of a majority of the outstanding shares, present in person or by proxy, constitute a quorum. The proxy materials were first made available to stockholders on April 2, 2026.

## Proposals

Proposal 1: Election of three Class III directors, Amara Nwosu, Robert Chen and Dr. Felix Adler, each to serve a three-year term expiring at the 2029 annual meeting. Directors are elected by a majority of the votes cast; a nominee who receives more votes against than for must tender his or her resignation to the Board. Proposal 2: Ratification of the appointment of Hartwell & Lowe LLP as the Company's independent registered public accounting firm for the fiscal year ending December 31, 2026. Proposal 3: Approval, on a non-binding advisory basis, of the compensation of the Company's named executive officers ("say-on-pay"). Proposal 4: Approval of the Northwind Analytics, Inc. 2026 Equity Incentive Plan, which reserves 6,500,000 shares of common stock for future awards and replaces the 2023 Equity Incentive Plan, under which 1,912,300 shares remained available as of the Record Date. Proposals 2, 3 and 4 require the affirmative vote of a majority of the votes cast. The Board recommends a vote FOR each nominee and FOR each of Proposals 2, 3 and 4. At the 2025 annual meeting, the say-on-pay proposal received the support of approximately 93% of the votes cast.

## Board of Directors and Corporate Governance

The Board consists of nine (9) directors, seven (7) of whom the Board has determined are independent under Nasdaq listing standards. Jonathan Mercer serves as Chairman and Chief Executive Officer, and Amara Nwosu serves as Lead Independent Director. The Board has three standing committees. The Audit Committee is chaired by Robert Chen, who the Board has determined is an audit committee financial expert, and its other members are Dr. Felix Adler and Maria Santangelo; it met eight (8) times in 2025. The Compensation Committee is chaired by Amara Nwosu and its other members are David Lindqvist and Maria Santangelo; it met five (5) times in 2025. The Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee is chaired by David Lindqvist. The Board met seven (7) times in 2025, and each director attended at least 90% of the meetings of the Board and the committees on which he or she served. Directors are expected to attend the annual meeting; all directors then serving attended the 2025 annual meeting.

## Director Compensation

Non-employee directors receive an annual cash retainer of $60,000, payable quarterly. The Lead Independent Director receives an additional $30,000. The chairs of the Audit Committee, Compensation Committee and Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee receive additional annual retainers of $20,000, $15,000 and $10,000 respectively, and other members of those committees receive $10,000, $7,500 and $5,000 respectively. Each non-employee director also receives an annual grant of restricted stock units with a grant date value of $180,000, which vests on the earlier of the first anniversary of the grant date and the next annual meeting. Non-employee directors may elect to receive their cash retainers in the form of deferred stock units. The Company's stock ownership guidelines require each non-employee director to hold shares with a value of five (5) times the annual cash retainer within five (5) years of joining the Board.

## Executive Compensation

The Compensation Committee's philosophy is to pay for performance, with a majority of target compensation for named executive officers delivered in equity. The annual cash bonus for 2025 was funded at 125% of target based on achievement of the Company's revenue and Adjusted EBITDA goals. Summary Compensation Table for fiscal 2025: Jonathan Mercer, Chairman and Chief Executive Officer: salary $550,000; bonus and non-equity incentive plan compensation $412,500; stock awards $4,200,000; all other compensation $51,800; total $5,214,300. Elena Vasquez, Chief Financial Officer (joined October 1, 2025): salary $106,250; signing bonus $75,000; non-equity incentive plan compensation $15,900; option awards $2,940,000, representing the grant date fair value of an option to purchase 300,000 shares; all other compensation $9,650; total $3,146,800. Priya Raman, Chief Revenue Officer: salary $400,000; non-equity incentive plan compensation $400,000; stock awards $1,800,000; all other compensation $18,400; total $2,618,400. Samuel Ortiz, Chief Technology Officer: salary $410,000; non-equity incentive plan compensation $256,250; stock awards $2,100,000; all other compensation $17,900; total $2,784,150. The Company does not provide tax gross-ups on change in control payments, and all equity awards to executive officers granted since the initial public offering are subject to the Company's Compensation Recovery Policy.

## Pay Ratio

The annual total compensation of the median employee, excluding the Chief Executive Officer, was $142,300 for fiscal 2025. The ratio of the Chief Executive Officer's annual total compensation of $5,214,300 to the median employee's compensation was approximately 37 to 1.

## Independent Registered Public Accounting Firm Fees

Fees billed by Hartwell & Lowe LLP for fiscal 2025 were: audit fees $1,420,000; audit-related fees $85,000, consisting of services related to the Bluewater Robotics acquisition; tax fees $210,000, consisting of tax compliance and advisory services; and all other fees $0; for a total of $1,715,000. Fees for fiscal 2024 totaled $1,510,000. The Audit Committee pre-approved all of these services.

## Security Ownership of Certain Beneficial Owners and Management

As of the Record Date, the following persons were known to the Company to beneficially own more than five percent (5%) of its common stock: Larkspur Capital Management LP, 8,713,400 shares, or 11.8%; Vantage Growth Partners, 6,941,100 shares, or 9.4%; and Meridian Index Advisors, 4,209,000 shares, or 5.7%. ## Voting and Attendance Information

Stockholders may vote by internet at www.proxyvote.example, by telephone at 1-800-555-0199, by mail using the enclosed proxy card, or during the virtual meeting by using the 16-digit control number included in the proxy materials. Proxies must be received by 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time on May 13, 2026 to be counted. A stockholder may revoke a proxy at any time before it is voted by submitting a later-dated proxy, by voting during the meeting, or by delivering written notice of revocation to the Corporate Secretary. Broker non-votes will be counted for quorum purposes but will not be counted as votes cast on Proposals 1, 3 and 4; brokers may vote uninstructed shares on Proposal 2 only. Stockholder proposals for inclusion in the proxy statement for the 2027 annual meeting must be received by the Corporate Secretary no later than December 3, 2026.
